flipper.

Chop it, flip it,
tap it.

A free sampler plug-in for people who can play with one sample all day. Tap along while it plays and it chops right there, and the slices land on the pads. Pitch it down and it tells you the tempo it lands on; change the engine and it takes on the grain of a late-80s or early-90s machine.

001 / HOW IT GOES

From one sample
to a beat

flipper chops a sample and flips the pieces into a new beat. It goes like this.

  1. 1

    Load

    Drop an audio file and the waveform comes up, with the sample's key. Set each sample to loop or one-shot.

  2. 2

    Chop

    Tap the pads along while it plays and it chops where you tap (lazy chop). Or let auto find the hits, cut it on a grid, place markers by hand, or roll the dice. Up to 64 slices.

  3. 3

    Play

    The slices land on the pads right away. Tap them, or spread one slice across the keyboard and play a melody. Pick a scale and only its notes are on the keys.

  4. 4

    Colour

    Change the engine for the grit of an old sampler, and pitch it down to a laid-back tempo. On the fx screen, add filter, tape and echo, and scratch the record.

  5. 5

    Take it out

    Drag the chop order into your DAW as MIDI, and save the whole thing as a .flip file. A bounce sounds exactly like what you heard.

002 / SCREENS

Three screens

The switch at the top moves between sampler, drum and fx. Whichever screen you look at, the same key plays the same sound.

sampler

Where you chop one sample and play it.

  • Tap to chop, or auto · grid · dice
  • 4×4 and 8×4 pads, and a 128-key melody
  • Pitch, volume, fades and channel (ST · L · R · M · S) per slice

drum

Where you build a kit from 16 one-shots.

  • Engine, trims, normalize and effects per cell
  • Choke groups and 16 outputs to your DAW
  • Save kits as .flipkit files and open them in other songs

fx

Where you grab the record and add effects.

  • An effect rack from filter to space
  • fx pads, touch strips and a fader
  • Scratch patterns like baby and chirp, and a rewind

003 / ENGINES

Change the engine,
change the sound and the look

Four engines. Each one changes the grain of the sound, and the screen changes into that engine's clothes. The three machine engines follow the way old samplers took sound in and played it out, down to the circuits, and each has its own filter, drive and record speed (33 · 45 · 78).

CLEAN · THE SAMPLE AS IT IS

The sample as it is. Stays clear however far you stretch it.

Reach for it when you want to keep the sound as it is. In keep-tempo mode it stays clean however much you change its length.

BLACK · 12-BIT · 26 kHz

Gritty and dark: the grain of a late-80s 12-bit sampler.

Reach for it when drums should hit heavy and dirty. Sample it in at a faster record speed and the top gets crunchier.

GREY · 12-BIT · 40 kHz

Tight and warm: the grain of a late-80s 12-bit sampler.

Reach for it when you want the kick and snare to sit tight and the whole loop to glue warm.

PURPLE · 4-BIT · 32 kHz

Soft and muffled: the 4-bit sound of an early-90s console.

Reach for it when you want it round and hazy. Lovely on keys and vocal chops.

004 / KEY & TEMPO

Pitch it down,
it tells you the tempo

In repitch mode, key and speed move together. Producers used to match it by ear against a metronome; flipper shows the BPM it lands on. In keep-tempo mode, key and tempo change apart.

You get 89.9 BPM
Pitch it by −5 st +2 ct

005 / MORE

And also

  • Key detection

    Load a sample and it finds the key. No more guessing by ear to match another sample.

  • Chop to MIDI

    Drag the chop order into your DAW as MIDI notes, then shuffle them into a new flip.

  • Melody

    Spread one slice across the keyboard and play it as a melody. Pick a scale and only its notes are on the keys.

  • Per slice

    Set pitch, volume, fades and channel (ST · L · R · M · S) for each slice.

  • Two modes

    Repitch moves key and speed together, the old way; keep tempo moves them apart. Pick per sample.

  • Scratch

    Grab the record and scratch, or play hand patterns like baby and chirp from one pad.

  • fx rack

    Filter, punch, distortion, crush, tape, chorus, noise, echo and space, chained in the order you pick.

  • MIDI learn

    Click a control, touch a knob or pad on your controller, done. An endless knob can rewind the record.

  • .flip files

    The sample, the chops and the settings in one file. Opens the same in another song or on another computer.

  • Undo

    Chops, moves and deletes can all be undone, so go ahead and press things.

  • Day and night

    One button switches between the day and night looks, and every engine has its own night look. Try it on this page too, at the top right.

  • English · 한국어

    Menus and help tips in both languages.

006 / INSTALL

Install

Mac

  • macOS, Apple Silicon and Intel (universal)
  • AU (Logic Pro and others) · VST3
  • The beta isn't signed by Apple yet, so macOS may block it the first time. Allow it once as the guide shows.

Windows

  • 64-bit Windows 10 · 11 (no ARM laptop build yet)
  • VST3
  • If SmartScreen warns you, click 'More info → Run anyway'.

Checked in Logic Pro, REAPER and Cubase. Most other DAWs that open AU or VST3 work too.

007 / BETA

It's a beta

Bug report

If something looks wrong, tell us with the bug report key at the bottom right of the plug-in. No name or account needed.

Update notice

When a new version is out, a small dot lights up by the logo. Click it to go and get it.

Nothing sent

The plug-in sends no usage data. A bug report goes out only when you press send and agree.

008 / FAQ

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. No money, no sign-up, and all of the source code is public.

Can I sell music I make with flipper?

Yes. The sounds and songs you make are yours. The licence covers the plug-in program, not what you make with it.

Will it run on my computer?

On Macs with Apple Silicon or Intel, and on 64-bit Windows 10 and 11. Not on ARM Windows yet.

Which DAWs?

Any DAW that opens AU or VST3 plug-ins. Checked in Logic Pro, REAPER and Cubase.

Where is the source code?

On GitHub. The code we wrote is MIT; the plug-in we ship is AGPL-3.0 because of the libraries bundled with it.

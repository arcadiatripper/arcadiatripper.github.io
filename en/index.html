What it does

  • 01 / 11

    Lazy chop

    Tap along. It chops where you tap.

  • 02 / 11

    Key detection

    It finds the sample's key, and how many cents it is off tune.

    Our own analyser (MIT): it matches the pitch-class profile (chroma) against the Albrecht–Shanahan key profiles, tuned on 158 key-labelled loops. A sound with no notes in it gets no key.

  • 03 / 11

    Repitch and keep tempo

    Repitch moves key and speed together, like the old samplers. Keep tempo moves the key only.

    Keep tempo is baked ahead with Rubber Band R3 (GPL); until the bake is ready you hear Signalsmith Stretch (MIT). Bounces use the same bake, so what you hear is what you get.

  • 04 / 11

    Crisp

    Hits stay sharp when keep tempo stretches. On for beats and drum loops, off for melodies, chords and voices.

    When on, each stretch between two hits is done on its own and every hit's attack keeps the original sound. Signalsmith Stretch (MIT).

  • 05 / 11

    Channel select

    Per sample: stereo, left, right, mid or side. Something you rarely find in other samplers.

    Mid = (L+R)/2, side = (L−R)/2. Picked where the sample comes in, before the engine, and never louder than the source. On the sampler screen.

  • 06 / 11

    Melody

    One slice across the keys, in the scale you pick.

  • 07 / 11

    Chop to MIDI

    Drag the chop order into your DAW.

  • 08 / 11

    Drum kit

    16 one-shots, each trimmed on its own.

  • 09 / 11

    fx rack

    Nine slots, from filter to space.

    Airwindows (MIT) models, plus our own punch, fuzz and filter.

  • 10 / 11

    Scratch

    Grab the record. Baby and chirp patterns too.

  • 11 / 11

    Day and night

    One button for the night look.

Change the engine, change the look

Two legendary samplers and an early-90s console's sound chip, modelled down to the circuits.

CLEAN · THE SAMPLE AS IT IS

The sample as it is. Clear however far you stretch it.

BLACK · 12-BIT · 26 kHz

The legendary late-80s sampler. Gritty and heavy.

Built to the schematics: how it stores 12 bits, pitch by skipping and repeating samples, a different filter on each output, and the moving filter, which was checked against recordings of a real unit.

GREY · 12-BIT · 40 kHz

The other legendary late-80s sampler. Tight and warm.

Little is published about it, so these are estimates: its 12-bit storage, how it changes pitch, the output filter's shape and the noise level. Recordings of a real unit will tune them.

PURPLE · 4-BIT · 32 kHz

An early-90s console's sound chip. Round and hazy.

The chip's maths (BRR decoding, gaussian interpolation, 32,040 Hz) matches the open-source emulator ares bit for bit, across 7,300 test cases. The board's filter follows the schematic. The one estimate: the encoder that turns a sample into the chip's format.

Shaped by hand, one decision at a time

flipper is not a copy of today's samplers. It was built by fixing, one at a time, the things that got in the way of making beats. These are those decisions.

01

Chop

Take one part of a sample and chop it until it sounds new. This is what flipper tries to do best.

  • Four ways: hits (move the sensitivity and it re-chops at once), beats (1/16 to a bar), lazy chop and the die.
  • Lazy chop: it plays, you tap a pad or any key, it cuts there. Tap a little early and it finds the hit from 80 ms before to 60 ms after; on real drum loops 90–100 % of taps land on their hit.
  • Lit pad: only the next slice's pad lights up and cuts, as on the old machines.
  • The die: a new chop on every roll. Drag to set the chaos, 0–100 %: 0 % picks only clear hits, evenly; 100 % is pure chance.
  • Skip: a count-in or a breath is left out, not cut out. The file is untouched and it can come back any time.
  • Up to 64 slices, and only five settings each: pitch, reverse, level, fade in, fade out.

“There was no sampler with a lazy chop. That's part of why I'm making this.”

maker's note

02

Key and tempo

Key and speed belong to the whole sample, not to each pad, so one sample can become a different track.

  • Repitch: like the old samplers, pitch it down and it slows down and darkens. No digital artefacts.
  • Keep tempo: the tempo stays and only the key moves. The sound is baked ahead, and you hear it at once while it bakes.
  • Pitch it down and it tells you the tempo you land on. Type the BPM you want and it tells you the semitones and cents. The sum producers used to do by ear against a metronome.
  • One DAW tempo key matches the song's tempo.
  • The key chip shows the sample's key and the key it sounds in with the key knob (e.g. Am → Gm).

03

Play

The sampler moves like one deck. The pads don't each go their own way.

  • 16 or 32 pads, with pages up to 64 slices. Pad 1 is C3.
  • Mono (the default): a new pad cuts the last sound in 5 ms, like a chop played on one deck. Poly: sounds overlap.
  • While held / whole slice: as long as you hold it, or on to the next marker. Either way a slice stops at its own end.
  • Melody: one slice across the keys, by semitone or in one of eight scales (major, minor, dorian, phrygian, mixolydian, harmonic minor, major and minor pentatonic). With the white-keys layout, every scale plays like C major.
  • The root isn't stuck on C3: move it, or right-click a key to make it the root.

“A diatonic scale is easier when you play it on a keyboard.”

maker's note

04

Bring a sample in

Drop a file, or record the DAW's sound straight in.

  • Record: another track (side-chain) or flipper's own output (resample). Now or from the next bar; until you stop, or 1, 2, 4 or 8 bars.
  • Channel select: stereo, left, right, mid or side. Mid keeps what sits in the centre, side keeps what is spread wide.
  • Whole sample gain: one sample's level, after the engine and before the fx. Normalize sets it.
  • File names in any language open.

05

fx and the turntable

The fx screen has the effect rack, and a turntable that plays the chopped range like a record.

  • Ten rack slots: filter, punch, distort, crush, tape, chorus, noise, echo, space, all. All off at first; turn a knob up and it comes on. An off slot uses no CPU.
  • Echoes and wobbles follow the DAW's beat, so a bounce sounds as it was heard.
  • fx pads: effects that last while you hold them.
  • The record: 33⅓ rpm. Grab it with the mouse or a controller and scratch. Hand moves are smoothed, so the needle never jumps.
  • Scratch patterns: baby, chirp, transformer, flare and more. 16 keys pick the slice; harder hits push further.
  • Follow the song: when the DAW plays, the record starts from its cue, in time.

06

Drums

A screen for building a kit from 16 one-shots, inside the same plug-in as the sampler.

  • Each cell: its own engine (clean, grey, black, purple), level, pan, pitch ±24, fades, choke group 1–4.
  • 16 outputs: send any cell to its own DAW track.
  • Echo and space: each cell sets only how much it sends; one heavy effect runs for all (CPU).
  • MIDI channel 10 from C1 plays cells 1–16. The zones are laid out so they never clash with the sampler's pads.
  • Save as a kit file (.flipkit) and open it in another project. Drop several files at once and they fill in order.

07

Out, and safe

What you make goes straight to the DAW, and nothing suddenly gets loud.

  • Chop to MIDI: a clip that plays each slice at the moment the loop reaches it, dragged onto a DAW track. Start from the sample's own groove.
  • Bounce = live: the same sound at block sizes from 1 to 4096, real time or offline.
  • MIDI learn: every control you perform with can be learned.
  • One .flip file holds the sample, the chop and the settings.
  • A sudden loud sound is treated as the worst bug: the causes are fixed and tests guard them. No limiter colours the everyday sound; the safety limiter at the very end (safe, vintage, off) is your choice.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. No money, no sign-up, and all of the source code is public.

Can I sell music I make with flipper?

Yes. The sounds and songs you make are yours. The licence covers the plug-in program, not what you make with it.

Will it run on my computer?

On Macs with Apple Silicon or Intel, and on 64-bit Windows 10 and 11. Not on ARM Windows yet.

Which DAWs?

Any DAW that opens AU or VST3 plug-ins. Checked in Logic Pro, REAPER and Cubase.

Anything to know about the beta?

macOS asks you to allow it once the first time; on Windows, click 'More info → Run anyway' in SmartScreen. When a new version is out, a dot lights up by the plug-in's logo.

Where is the source code?

On GitHub. The code we wrote is MIT; the plug-in we ship is AGPL-3.0 because of the libraries bundled with it.
